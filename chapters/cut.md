To answer these questions, I compare spectral features across the Frauscher iEEG atlas and source-reconstructed HD-EEG data in four stages.

First, oscillatory features are described phenomenologically in both modalities using two complementary descriptors. The first is a data-driven clustering of the raw normalised spectra (Frauscher-style), which identifies regional peaks without imposing canonical bands, allowing the recovery of "natural" peak frequencies. This descriptor is unstable: the clustering solution and the no-peak reference set are sensitive to the chosen fitting range, and the cluster identities are not stable across reruns. The instability is reported as a finding rather than treated as a nuisance. The second descriptor is the per-region per-band peak prevalence from specparam, which is more stable but imposes the canonical band structure.

Second, cross-modal correspondence is computed on the uncorrected, relative-power PSDs. The Afnan band-overlap metric is applied region by region and band by band, and the per-region correlation between iEEG and HD-EEG is also computed <!-- in the manner of @janiukstyte2023NormativeBrainMapping (their figure 3A).  -->This yields the baseline against which the aperiodic-corrected results are contrasted.

Third, the aperiodic component is modelled and removed. Specparam is fit in both knee and fixed mode to each modality over 0.5-80 Hz or 1-80Hz frequency range, and the mode used per modality is selected by BIC. The selection is reported alongside prior evidence on the spectral shape in each modality, since BIC penalises the extra knee parameter and the choice can depend on the number of fitted bins. The fitted aperiodic component is subtracted in linear power space as the primary residual; the log-ratio subtraction used by specparam is a methodological alternative that may produce different overlap values, and is reported as a sensitivity comparison where time permits. The  metric  of overlap defined by @afnan2024ValidationMEGSource  <!-- and the Janiukstyte-style correlation are --> recomputed on the corrected spectra and compared against the uncorrected baseline.

Fourth, the stability of the aperiodic exponent is assessed. For each modality, the fixed-mode exponent is computed over both 1-80 Hz and 1-45 Hz, and the per-channel agreement between the two is quantified. I The resulting exponent values  comparisons are pooled into a hierarchical Bayesian regression with ROI and subject as random effects, with fitting range and decomposition method as fixed-effect predictors, to localise where the choice of range or method materially shifts the estimate.

A following analyses, IRASA exponents, restricted by the Nyquist limit to 2-40 Hz in HD-EEG, are compared with specparam fixed-mode exponents fit over the same restricted band.

There are some limitations in the realisation of this approach. The Frauscher iEEG atlas was recorded with eyes closed, whereas the HD-EEG dataset used here was recorded with eyes open. Eye state affects oscillatory activity, most strongly posterior alpha power, and may also affect aperiodic exponent and offset [@geller2014EyeClosureCauses]. Where cross-modal agreement is observed, it is therefore observed despite differences in recording condition. Where cross-modal agreement is weak, modality and condition cannot be fully separated with the data used here. This limitation is considered again in the Discussion
